Harvard Bioscience, Inc. · Nasdaq: HBIO

Instruments for life science research, since 1901.

Harvard Bioscience describes itself as “a leading developer, manufacturer and seller of technologies, products and services that enable fundamental advances in life science applications”. It sells to academic and government laboratories, to pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies and to contract research organizations, from operations in the United States, Europe and China. Story

From the syringe pump to the organoid plate.

The business began in 1901 as Harvard Apparatus, founded by Dr. William T. Porter, a professor of physiology at Harvard Medical School. Its early inventions included ventilators, the mechanical syringe pump for drug infusion in the 1950s and the microprocessor-controlled syringe pump in the 1980s. Filed 10-K

Its products and services are sold “to customers ranging from renowned academic institutions and government laboratories to the world’s leading pharmaceutical, biotechnology and contract research organizations (‘CROs’),” and through “a combination of direct and distribution channels” to customers around the world. Filed 10-K

“As the life sciences industry accelerates toward New Approach Methodologies (“NAMs”), Harvard Bioscience expects to evolve from a traditional tools provider into a leading enabler of Translational Medicine – positioned to bridge the gap between laboratory research and human clinical success.”

Company statement 10-K, Item 1

Plan

The 10-K sets out four pillars.

These are the company’s own words about what it plans to do. They are intentions, not results. Company statement

  1. 01

    Leading the Translational Bridge

    The company plans to lead by “bridging in vivo and in vitro research” and to help the industry move into “the organoid and 3D biology markets,” offering customers “an integrated solution across critical stages of discovery and development.”

  2. 02

    New Product Introduction pipeline

    Platforms such as SoHo™ telemetry, and the proprietary MeshMEA and Incub8 platforms, “both of which are designed for organoid and tissue recording.”

  3. 03

    Consumables Revenue Expansion

    It aims at “shifting mix toward higher-margin consumables and software,” starting from what it calls recurring revenue, 55% of total revenues at December 31, 2025. The 10-K does not define the measure, and the company’s 2026 investor presentations give 54% for 2025.

  4. 04

    Operational Excellence and Disciplined Growth

    Using the preclinical business “as a cash-generating foundation” for research and development and bolt-on acquisitions. Project Viking, approved January 23, 2026, is to close the Holliston, Massachusetts manufacturing facility and move U.S. production to Minneapolis, with some operations moving to Germany, Sweden and the UK. Holliston stays open through 2026, and the 8-K expects the consolidation to be completed by the first quarter of 2027. The company expects about $3 million of savings in 2027 and about $4 million a year from 2028; the 10-Q estimates restructuring charges of about $3.6 to $4.5 million, including $0.6 to $0.7 million of non-cash charges, subject to change. 8-K, Jan 29, 2026

10-K, Item 1 · the savings figures are the company’s expectations in its January 29, 2026 8-K

Financing and listing

What the filings say about the debt and the listing.

Set out in the filings’ words, with the dates. Nothing here is softened and nothing is added.

Going-concern history

The 10-K says the anticipated maturity of the old credit agreement on December 22, 2025, with uncertainty about future covenants, “raised substantial doubt about the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern.” After the December 17, 2025 refinancing, management “concluded that (1) the conditions and events that initially raised substantial doubt have been alleviated and (2) substantial doubt does not exist as of the issuance date.” Filed 10-K

Interest is the greater of 12.80% (then 12.50% after the second anniversary) and the prime rate plus 5.25%, paid monthly in cash; the company may pay interest on Term C in kind. Quarterly principal payments on Terms A and B start December 31, 2027. A 10.00% exit fee is payable on term loan amounts that are prepaid or repaid, including at maturity, except Term C amounts that convert into common stock. Prepayment premiums are 3.00% in the first year, 2.00% in the second and 1.00% in the third, and none after that; no premium is due on Term A prepaid before March 31, 2027. Term C may not be prepaid before maturity except on full repayment of all term loans or a change of control. Maturity and the start of amortisation may be extended by one year if the company meets an adjusted EBITDA milestone. Filed 8-K, Dec 17, 2025

The 10-Q gives an effective interest rate of 17.9% for the second quarter of 2026. After the reverse split Term C converts at $10.00 a share, at the lenders’ option or automatically if the stock exceeds $15.00 for thirty consecutive trading days: about 750,000 shares, or 16.5% of the 4,552,305 shares outstanding, before any interest paid in kind Computed. The lenders also hold detachable warrants for 200,000 shares at $5.00 a share, exercisable for seven years from December 17, 2025; 30,000 were exercised in the first quarter of 2026. 10-Q Filed 8-K, Dec 17, 2025

Covenants

The loan agreement has a minimum liquidity covenant that applies at all times and a minimum adjusted EBITDA covenant tested at the end of each quarter. The 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 says the company was in compliance with these covenants, including a $3.0 million minimum liquidity requirement and a minimum adjusted EBITDA of at least $6.0 million on a trailing 12-month basis. Filed 10-Q

The Nasdaq minimum bid price, in order

  1. Apr 4, 2025
    Nasdaq’s notification letter: the bid price had been below $1.00 for 30 consecutive business days, February 21 to April 3, 2025.
  2. Oct 3, 2025
    The stock moved from the Nasdaq Global Market to the Nasdaq Capital Market, with a further 180 days, to March 30, 2026, to regain compliance.
  3. Mar 6, 2026
    Stockholders approved a reverse stock split at a special meeting.
  4. Mar 13, 2026
    A 1-for-10 reverse split took effect. The 10-K, filed the same day, says “We cannot provide any guarantee that we will be able to regain or maintain compliance with Nasdaq’s listing requirements in the future.”
  5. Mar 16, 2026
    The 10-Q reports that the stock began trading on a split-adjusted basis on the Nasdaq Capital Market, still as HBIO.
